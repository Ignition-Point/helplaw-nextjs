What Are Your Legal Rights?
Families and victims of nursing home sexual abuse in California have significant civil options under state law, independent of any criminal prosecution.

The Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act (EADACPA)
Enacted in 1991, this California law was specifically designed to encourage private civil enforcement of elder abuse laws by providing enhanced remedies. Under EADACPA, if a plaintiff can prove by clear and convincing evidence that a facility or caregiver acted with recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice, the law allows recovery of attorney's fees and costs. It also allows recovery of damages for the victim's pre-death pain and suffering — even if the victim has passed away. This is a significant departure from standard California personal injury law, which typically limits recovery after death.

Civil vs. Criminal
The criminal prosecution of Lumbi and a civil lawsuit are separate proceedings. A civil lawsuit can proceed regardless of the outcome of the criminal case. The standard of proof in a civil case is lower than in a criminal case, which means families can pursue civil claims even if Lumbi is acquitted or if the criminal case is resolved through a plea agreement. A conviction, however, significantly strengthens a civil claim.

Statute of Limitations
For personal injury or physical/sexual abuse under California Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1, a civil lawsuit must generally be filed within two years of the date of the injury. Given that the alleged assaults occurred between March and July 2024, filing deadlines for civil claims may begin as early as March 2026. Prompt legal review is important to preserve all available options.

Mandated Reporting Failures
California Welfare and Institutions Code § 15630 designates nursing home employees, administrators, and healthcare professionals as mandated reporters. They are required by law to immediately report any known or suspected instances of elder abuse. If Forest Hill Manor staff were aware of the abuse and failed to report it, both the individuals who failed to report and the facility itself could face additional criminal and civil liability.
