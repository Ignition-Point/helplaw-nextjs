The FTCA Process: What You Need to Know

The Federal Tort Claims Act is the legal mechanism by which the government can be held liable for negligence by federal employees. Here's how it works:

Step 1: Administrative Complaint
Before filing a lawsuit, you must file an administrative claim with the appropriate federal agency (in this case, the Department of Defense). This claim must describe the harm, the date of the incident, and the compensation sought. There is a strict deadline: two years from the date you discovered (or reasonably should have discovered) the injury.

This deadline is not forgiving. If you suspect you may be affected, consultation with an attorney is critical.

Step 2: Government Response
The government has six months to respond to your administrative claim. They will either deny it, settle it, or let the clock run, after which you can proceed to federal court.

Step 3: Federal Court (if necessary)
If the administrative claim is denied or not resolved, you can file suit in federal district court. These cases are heard by judges (not juries). The standard of proof is "preponderance of the evidence"—more likely than not.

Why This Matters for Your Case

FTCA claims require understanding these deadlines and procedures. The statute of limitations is strict. A delay in filing your administrative claim can permanently bar your case. This is not a case where attorneys can negotiate a filing deadline extension.

Additionally, FTCA claims are limited. The government is not liable for punitive damages (damages meant to punish). Compensation is limited to documented economic losses (medical expenses, lost wages) and pain and suffering. Some states have caps on pain and suffering in similar claims.

The government has sovereign immunity. You cannot sue a military commander personally under FTCA. You sue the United States. This means the litigation strategy and the institutional structure of the case are different from a typical medical malpractice lawsuit.

What Reaching Out Does and Does Not Do
