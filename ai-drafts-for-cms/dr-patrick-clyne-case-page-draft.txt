Sexual abuse by a physician carries a specific kind of harm. The abuse is disguised as care, which can make it difficult for survivors to name what happened to them — sometimes for years. Common effects include:

* Difficulty trusting medical professionals, leading to avoidance of necessary healthcare

What Are Your Legal Rights?

California has significantly expanded the legal options available to survivors of childhood sexual abuse in recent years. Understanding the current law starts with the changes that made these claims possible.

The California Child Victims Act (AB 218)

Enacted in 2019, this law expanded the statute of limitations for childhood sexual abuse claims. Survivors can now file civil lawsuits until their 40th birthday or within five years of discovering that a psychological injury was caused by childhood sexual abuse — whichever is later. AB 218 also created a three-year lookback window (2020-2022) that allowed previously time-barred claims to be filed, and introduced treble damages against institutions that covered up abuse.

Assembly Bill 452

Passed in the 2023-2024 legislative session, AB 452 eliminated the statute of limitations entirely for civil lawsuits regarding childhood sexual assault that occurred on or after January 1, 2024.

A survivor can pursue a civil claim even if no criminal charges were ever filed. Civil cases operate under a lower burden of proof — preponderance of the evidence rather than beyond a reasonable doubt — which means survivors can seek accountability and compensation through the civil courts regardless of what happened in the criminal system.

Filing deadlines can change. Eligibility depends on individual circumstances, and understanding how the current laws apply to your situation starts with a direct conversation.

What Compensation Can Survivors Recover?

California does not cap damages in sexual assault and abuse cases. A civil lawsuit may seek compensation for:

* Medical expenses, including therapy and ongoing mental health treatment
* Lost earning capacity and disrupted career development
* Pain and suffering
* Emotional distress
* Punitive damages, designed to punish particularly egregious conduct
* Treble damages under AB 218 against institutions that engaged in a cover-up

Civil cases also serve a broader purpose. Through the discovery process, lawsuits can compel institutions to disclose what they knew, when they knew it, and what they failed to do. For many survivors, this institutional accountability is as important as the financial recovery.
